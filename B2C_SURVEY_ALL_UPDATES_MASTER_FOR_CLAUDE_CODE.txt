B2C SURVEY — MASTER UPDATE & VALIDATION RULES
==============================================
FOR CLAUDE CODE

PURPOSE
-------
This file combines ALL B2C survey updates agreed so far.
Apply these rules during generation AND run them again as automated validation
before the survey is finalized.

The goal is not to make B2C surveys look like B2B surveys. B2C attitudinal,
behavioral and psychographic questions may legitimately cover different angles
of the same product/category. Remove only questions that test essentially the
same construct from the same angle or that fail the value test.

====================================================================
1. EXACTLY FOUR MAIN B2C BEHAVIORAL SECTIONS
====================================================================

The B2C Consumer Goods structure uses these four main behavioral sections:

1. Consumer Profile, Ownership & Usage Behavior
2. Purchase Journey & Decision Drivers
3. Brand / Product Experience & Satisfaction
4. Unmet Needs, Switching & Future Purchase Intent

These are the FOUR main behavioral sections.

Profiling & Classification is NOT a fifth behavioral segment.
Age, gender, household composition and household income may appear in a final
Profiling block when required, but they are classification variables only and
must be asked at the end.

IMPORTANT ABOUT THE SLASH:
--------------------------
The slash in "Brand / Product Experience & Satisfaction" is OPTIONAL.
It is not mandatory to use "/". It is acceptable to render this as, for example,
"Brand & Product Experience & Satisfaction" or another natural market/language
form, provided that it still clearly represents the same underlying segment.
Do NOT create an additional segment just because the slash is removed.

Do not add a fifth behavioral section.
Do not revert to old names such as:
- Consumer Profile
- Buying Behavior
- Preferences & Expectations
- Satisfaction & Future Intent
unless a market-specific configuration explicitly requires a natural localized
label. The underlying four-part structure must remain intact.

====================================================================
2. EXACTLY FOUR BEHAVIORAL PERSONAS / SEGMENT NAMES IN DISTRIBUTION NOTES
====================================================================

Use exactly these four behavioral personas throughout distribution notes:

1. High-Involvement Users
2. Targeted Concern Solvers
3. Value-Driven Routine Users
4. Minimalist Occasional Users

STRICT RULES:
- These four names are the only behavioral persona names allowed unless a
  market-specific input explicitly overrides them.
- Spell the names consistently everywhere.
- Never introduce a fifth or sixth persona halfway through the survey.
- Never rename an existing persona in one question.
- Never use shortened variants such as "High-Involvement" or "Value-Driven"
  as if they were separate persona names.
- Never create personas from demographics, income, gender, age, household type,
  geography, or other profiling attributes.
- Never use names such as "Premium-Luxury Buyers", "Clean-Conscious Shoppers",
  "Sensitive-Skin Sufferers", "Budget Consumers", "Younger Users",
  "Family-Oriented Consumers", etc. unless explicitly added to the approved
  persona set by a separate market-specific configuration.

Before finalizing, scan every distribution note and extract all persona names.
The set must contain exactly the four approved names above.

A persona must also behave consistently. If a persona is described as
price-sensitive in one question, do not describe the same persona as strongly
premium-seeking in another question without a real analytical reason.

====================================================================
3. B2C VS B2B — DO NOT APPLY THE WRONG REDUNDANCY RULE
====================================================================

B2C questions may probe different psychological/business constructs even when
answer options overlap.

Examples of legitimate distinct B2C constructs:
- usage frequency
- routine length
- purchase trigger
- research source
- purchase channel
- final decision driver
- product must-have
- quality cue
- claim credibility
- retention condition
- satisfaction
- disappointment
- unmet need
- switching trigger
- advocacy
- future intent
- willingness to pay

Keep two questions when they clearly support different business decisions,
such as positioning, formulation, messaging, acquisition, retention or
channel strategy.

REMOVE or MERGE a question only when:
- it tests essentially the same construct as another question;
- it asks from the same angle;
- it produces no additional actionable insight;
- or it is not required for a clear business decision.

For every question ask:
1. What business decision does it support?
2. Is that decision already covered elsewhere?
3. Is it actionable enough?
4. Is it genuinely B2C?
5. Does it fit the section where it appears?

If there is no good answer, remove the question.

====================================================================
4. QUESTION WORDING — DIRECT, NATURAL, SIMPLE
====================================================================

This is a HARD RULE.

Questions must sound like something a normal consumer would naturally answer.

DO NOT unnecessarily start questions with:
- "In the past year, ..."
- "In the last 12 months, ..."
- "Thinking about the past year, ..."
- "When considering ..."
- "If you think about ..."
- "For yourself, ..."
- "Personally, ..."
- "In your opinion, ..."
- other setup phrases that add no meaning.

Example:
BAD: "In the past year, how often have you used skin care products?"
BETTER: "How often do you usually use skin care products?"

BAD: "In the past year, what most often disappointed you about a skin care product?"
BETTER: "What most often disappoints you about skin care products?"

A timeframe should be included ONLY when it is analytically necessary.
If a timeframe is genuinely required, keep it short and natural.

GENERAL STYLE RULES:
- Lead with the actual question.
- Use everyday consumer language.
- Keep wording short.
- Avoid unnecessary setup clauses.
- Avoid business/consulting jargon.
- Avoid filler such as "for you", "personally", "in your opinion" unless
  removing it changes the meaning.
- Use active voice.
- Use complete natural sentences.
- Do not use fragments.
- Every answer option must sound like something a real person would naturally say.
- Do not make options sound like internal database labels.

====================================================================
5. ONE QUESTION = ONE DIMENSION
====================================================================

This is mandatory.

A single-select question must measure one clear dimension.
Do not mix time-of-day, occasion, trigger, reason, frequency, motivation,
product type, or other dimensions in the same answer list.

BAD:
"When do you usually apply skin care products?"
Options:
- Morning
- Evening
- Both morning and evening
- When my skin feels dry
- Before going out

This mixes TIME with TRIGGER/OCCASION.

Correct approach: choose ONE dimension.

Example TIME question:
"When do you usually apply skin care products?"
- Morning
- Evening
- Both morning and evening
- No set time

OR a TRIGGER question:
"When are you most likely to apply skin care products?"
- As part of my morning routine
- As part of my evening routine
- After showering or bathing
- When my skin feels dry or uncomfortable
- Before going out or for special occasions

Do not combine both.

====================================================================
6. QUESTION TYPES MUST BE EXPLICIT AND CORRECT
====================================================================

Every question must have an explicit backend type:
- single-select
- multi-select
- ranking
- rating scale

Never make the reader infer type from percentages or wording.

SINGLE-SELECT:
- respondent chooses exactly one answer;
- all options must describe the same dimension;
- options should be mutually exclusive.

MULTI-SELECT:
- question wording must explicitly say "Select all that apply.";
- options may coexist;
- use a genuine opt-out where appropriate.

RATING SCALE:
- options must form a clear ordered scale;
- wording must be consistent from low to high.

RANKING:
- only use a ranking question if the backend actually supports ranking;
- ranking output must define what the displayed percentages mean;
- do NOT show arbitrary percentages against ranked options and imply that
  they are ordinary single-select shares.

If ranking support is weak, replace a ranking question with:
"Which factor has the biggest influence on your final choice?"

====================================================================
7. MULTI-SELECT OPT-OUT RULE
====================================================================

For multi-select questions asking about must-haves, requirements, concerns,
preferences or similar constructs, include a genuine opt-out when a respondent
may honestly have none.

Examples:
- "None of these are must-haves for me"
- "None of these matter to me"
- "None of these"

Do not force a low-involvement/minimalist respondent to select an attribute
that does not actually matter to them.

If the system supports validation logic, the opt-out should be mutually
exclusive with substantive choices.

====================================================================
8. ANSWER OPTION VALIDATION
====================================================================

Every question must pass all of these checks:
- options answer the exact same question;
- single-select options are mutually exclusive;
- multi-select options can logically coexist;
- categories do not overlap unnecessarily;
- options are complete enough for normal respondents;
- wording is natural;
- no clipped/incomplete phrases;
- no internal category labels;
- no contradictory choices;
- no missing honest opt-out where required;
- percentages match the declared question type.

For single-select questions, ask:
"Could one real respondent reasonably fit more than one option?"
If yes, redesign the options.

====================================================================
9. FIXED QUESTION-QUALITY RULES FROM THE PRIOR REVIEW
====================================================================

The following recurring defects must never reappear:

A. MIXED DIMENSION OPTIONS
Do not mix time-of-day with occasion/trigger in one single-select question.

B. WRONG RELATIONSHIP REGISTER
For consumer personal-care/household categories, recommendation questions
should use natural relationships such as "friend or family member" rather than
B2B-style "friend or colleague".

C. SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT
Example:
WRONG: "Which aspects ... most disappoints you?"
RIGHT: "Which aspects ... most disappoint you?"

Run a grammar check, especially when the subject is plural.

D. NEAR-IDENTICAL WORDING WITH DIFFERENT TYPES
If one question is multi-select and another is single-select, the wording must
make the distinction obvious.
Example:
"Select all that apply." must appear in the multi-select version.

E. NO FORCED ANSWERS
Include "None of these" or equivalent when appropriate.

F. NO MALFORMED PUNCTUATION
Never output:
"Select all that apply.?"
Correct it to:
"Select all that apply."

====================================================================
10. SEGMENT PLACEMENT RULE
====================================================================

A question can be non-redundant and still be in the wrong segment.

Check whether its analytical angle matches the section:

SEGMENT 1 — Consumer Profile, Ownership & Usage Behavior
Use for who uses/buys the category and how they use it:
usage frequency, routine, product usage context, usage purpose, household use.

SEGMENT 2 — Purchase Journey & Decision Drivers
Use for purchase mechanics:
purchase trigger, research, discovery, channel, replenishment, spend,
comparison, decision drivers.

SEGMENT 3 — Brand / Product Experience & Satisfaction
Use for product/brand expectations and evaluation:
must-haves, quality cues, claims, sensory experience, packaging when relevant,
trade-offs, price expectations, perceived value.

SEGMENT 4 — Unmet Needs, Switching & Future Purchase Intent
Use for:
satisfaction, disappointment, unmet needs, switching, loyalty/advocacy,
repurchase, future intent and future spending behavior.

Do not put a purchase-mechanics question into a product-perception section,
or a satisfaction question into a purchase-trigger section, just because the
answer options overlap.

====================================================================
11. CURRENTLY KNOWN HIGH-VALUE QUESTION DECISIONS
====================================================================

These are examples from the reviewed Skin Care Products survey and should guide
the generator when the same patterns appear again.

Q1 — GENERAL USAGE / ROUTINE COUNT
Keep if it measures a clear usage dimension.
Natural wording example:
"How many skin care products do you use on a typical day?"
Avoid unnecessary time setup.

Q2 — NARROW SINGLE-PRODUCT SUBTYPE USAGE
A general skincare survey should not over-focus on one product subtype unless
that subtype is an explicit market objective.
Replace or broaden if it is only asking cleanser-specific behavior.

Q3 — SKIN CONCERN
A question asking the single most important/bothersome concern is useful if
Q4-type questions separately measure the full range of concerns.
Keep a "None of these" option where appropriate.

Q4 — ROUTINE STEPS
Useful as a multi-select if it measures breadth of routine.
Phrase cleanly:
"Which steps are part of your usual skin care routine? Select all that apply."

PURCHASE TRIGGER vs RECENT PURCHASE:
A question about what caused the most recent purchase and a question about
future purchase triggers can both stay because they measure different things.

RESEARCH / DISCOVERY:
"Where do you usually learn about..." and "Which sources do you use to compare..."
can both stay only if they clearly measure discovery vs comparison.

PACKAGING:
Do not create multiple packaging questions that ask the same basic preference.
Keep packaging only when it supports a real product/packaging decision.
Specify the product context when packaging depends strongly on product type.

PRODUCT FORMAT:
A broad "Which skin care formats are you willing to use?" question is often
low-value and can be redundant with product-usage questions. Remove it unless
there is an explicit market-specific reason to measure format adoption.

MUST-HAVE vs RETENTION:
"What must a product have/be for me to buy it?" and "What must it do for me to
keep using it?" can be legitimate separate B2C constructs. Do not delete one
automatically.

QUALITY CUE:
"What tells you a product is high quality before you buy it?" is useful and
distinct from must-haves and claim persuasion.

CLAIM PERSUASION:
A question such as "Which single claim most strongly convinces you to try a
new product?" is useful.
Avoid keeping another near-identical question about the same exact claim unless
there is a clear separate analytical purpose.

TRADE-OFF:
A "what would you give up to pay less?" question is useful only if all answer
options belong to the same conceptual dimension and each is a plausible trade-off.
Do not mix product attributes with unrelated recommendation/channel concepts.

SATISFACTION:
Overall satisfaction is distinct from satisfaction drivers.

DISAPPOINTMENT / UNMET NEED:
A disappointment question is distinct from overall satisfaction and can be
retained when it provides actionable unmet-need detail.

SWITCHING:
A switching trigger is a distinct business decision from satisfaction.

ADVOCACY:
A recommendation/NPS-style item can be retained if useful. For personal-care
products use natural consumer relationships such as "friend or family member".

FUTURE INTENT:
Future purchase/routine intent can be retained when it supports a separate
forecasting or innovation decision.

====================================================================
12. CURRENCY AND REGIONAL CONSISTENCY — HARD RULE
====================================================================

Every geography-bearing field must match the survey's stated market/region.

If Region = India:
- every price, spend and income question must use INR;
- use a valid rendered rupee symbol: ₹;
- use India-appropriate price/income bands;
- do not default to USD;
- do not reuse US/Europe price bands blindly.

NEVER output broken forms such as:
- n500
- n1,000
- n25,000
- $500 in an India survey
- broken replacement characters

Before finalizing, run an encoding check over every monetary option label.

Also check all geography-bearing metadata fields for contradictions.
If the header says India, another metadata block must not say Global or another
region unless the survey is explicitly configured that way.

====================================================================
13. MONTHLY SPEND VS HOUSEHOLD INCOME
====================================================================

These are different constructs and may both exist.

Monthly category spend = behavioral/purchase measure.
Household income = profiling/classification measure.

Do not use the income answer to create a behavioral finding.

Household income bands must be realistic for the stated country and target
population. Do not use implausibly low bands that contradict the survey's own
audience description.

====================================================================
14. PROFILING RULES
====================================================================

Age, gender, household composition and income belong in the final profiling
block when the survey requires them.

They are used for cross-tabulation, NOT qualification/gating unless the market
configuration explicitly requires a qualification criterion.

Do NOT attribute behavioral findings to profiling answers inside the profiling
question's distribution note.

BAD:
"Younger High-Involvement Users skew the panel toward 18-34."

BAD:
"Value-Driven Routine Users cluster at lower income levels."

BAD:
"Family-oriented segments drive the largest share living with children."

Preferred:
- no distribution note for profiling questions; OR
- a neutral descriptive note only, with no behavioral persona attribution.

Do not invent demographic personas such as:
- Younger Users
- Family-Oriented Consumers
- Premium Users
- Budget Consumers
- Female-led Segments
- Middle-Income Shoppers

====================================================================
15. DISTRIBUTION NOTES — STRICT RULES
====================================================================

Distribution notes must:
- use only the four approved persona names;
- be consistent with the question and answer options;
- explain meaningful skew or pattern, not just repeat the options;
- avoid invented causes;
- avoid demographic interpretation unless the note is outside profiling and the
  survey methodology explicitly supports such an inference;
- never introduce a new persona name.

Do not write unsupported causal claims such as:
"Growth in the region pushes spending upward"
or
"Social media causes this segment to spend more"
unless the source/model explicitly supports that causal statement.

Safer form:
"High-Involvement Users and Targeted Concern Solvers are more represented in
the higher-spend bands in the modeled distribution."

Distribution notes should describe the modeled distribution, not invent causal
stories.

====================================================================
16. QUESTION TYPE + PERCENTAGE CONSISTENCY
====================================================================

The survey disclosure may say:
- single-choice, ranking and rating-scale questions sum to 100%;
- multi-select questions do not.

But this only works if the actual question type matches the wording and data.

Validation:
- Single-select percentages must sum to 100%.
- Rating-scale percentages must sum to 100%.
- A true ranking must use a valid ranking calculation; do not fake it as simple
  single-choice percentages.
- Multi-select percentages may exceed 100% in total.

Never let a question's answer type be inferred by looking at percentages.
The backend must declare the type explicitly.

====================================================================
17. STRUCTURE / QUESTION COUNT RECONCILIATION
====================================================================

The survey header and actual body must be generated from the same source data.

Before finalizing:
1. Count every question under every section.
2. Confirm each section's stated question count matches the body.
3. Confirm the four behavioral section counts add correctly.
4. Confirm the profiling count matches the actual profiling block.
5. Confirm the grand total matches the actual total.
6. Confirm section names are consistent with the structure.

Do not write a structure summary independently from the generated body.

====================================================================
18. QUESTION COUNT IS NOT A GOAL BY ITSELF
====================================================================

Do not add questions merely to hit a target count.
Do not keep a weak question just because the structure says a section should
have a certain number.

If a question is not needed, remove it and let the final count reflect the real
survey design. If a specific configuration requires a fixed count, only fill the
count with questions that pass the value test.

====================================================================
19. PLAIN-LANGUAGE + ACCURACY STANDARD
====================================================================

Apply the same strict simplicity and accuracy standard used for B2B work, while
keeping B2C vocabulary consumer-friendly.

Rules:
- lead with the actual question;
- cut unnecessary setup;
- use active voice;
- use complete sentences;
- remove filler;
- use ordinary consumer terms;
- every question must precisely describe what it measures;
- every answer option must be a complete natural phrase;
- simplicity must not reduce accuracy.

====================================================================
20. MARKET-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
====================================================================

A question may be removed, broadened or added only when justified by the market,
category or explicit survey input.

Do not assume that every skincare market needs packaging, devices, premium tiers,
clinical products, influencer questions, etc.

The question must fit the actual category and the stated market.

====================================================================
22. IMPORTANT: APPLY RULES DURING GENERATION, NOT ONLY AFTER
====================================================================

Do not rely only on a post-generation cleanup pass.

The generator itself must:
- select the four sections before writing questions;
- define the four approved personas before generating distribution notes;
- assign every question a type before generating answer options;
- generate options that match that type;
- generate wording naturally;
- apply regional currency before output;
- keep profiling separate;
- create the structure summary from the actual generated sections;
- run the full automated validation before final output.

If any validation check fails, regenerate/fix the affected question before
returning the survey.

====================================================================
23. FINAL B2C QUALITY TEST
====================================================================

Before accepting a generated question, ask:

1. Is this question genuinely required?
2. What real business decision will it support?
3. Is that decision already covered elsewhere?
4. Is the question simple enough for an ordinary consumer?
5. Is the wording natural without unnecessary setup or timeframe?
6. Does the answer set measure exactly one dimension?
7. Is the declared question type correct?
8. Are the options mutually exclusive where required?
9. Is there an honest opt-out where appropriate?
10. Does the question belong in the selected segment?
11. Does the distribution note use only an approved persona?
12. Does the distribution note logically follow from the answers?
13. Does it avoid unsupported causal claims?
14. Is regional/currency information correct?
15. Is there any simpler or more accurate way to ask the same thing?

If the answer to any of these is NO, fix the question before output.

====================================================================
24. IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE
====================================================================

The goal is NOT to make every B2C survey contain fewer questions.
The goal is to make every remaining question:
- necessary,
- simple,
- accurate,
- analytically useful,
- correctly typed,
- correctly segmented,
- regionally consistent,
- and supported by valid answer options.

Different B2C questions can legitimately overlap in vocabulary while measuring
different constructs. Preserve those when they support different decisions.
Remove only genuinely redundant or low-value questions.

25. GAPS FOUND IN THE MASTER RULES FILE ITSELF
(found on review, before the Running Shoes run — these are gaps in the
RULES, not in a specific survey)
====================================================================

25a. SLASH-HANDLING RULE CONTRADICTS THE B2B PRECEDENT
Section 1 says the slash in "Brand / Product Experience & Satisfaction"
is optional formatting and can become "Brand & Product Experience" —
i.e. KEEP both words. But the B2B rule set (switchgear/thermal power
surveys) treated a slash as "pick ONE word" (e.g. "Technology /
Solution Adoption" became "Technology Adoption," dropping "Solution").
These are opposite resolutions of the same symbol.
FIX: State explicitly that B2C segment-name slashes mean "both terms
apply, join naturally" while B2B segment-name slashes mean "choose the
one term that matches the segment's actual question content." Do not
apply the B2B slash rule to B2C segment names, or vice versa.

25b. NO AUTOMATED CHECK FOR THE MULTI-SELECT OPT-OUT RULE
Section 7 mandates a genuine opt-out ("None of these") on must-have/
attribute multi-select questions. No check number in Section 21's list
of 35 verifies this exists. A rule with no matching check will not be
enforced by the "run all checks before output" process.
FIX: Add to the automated check list:
"CHECK 36 — Every multi-select question about must-haves, requirements,
concerns, or preferences includes a genuine opt-out option where a
respondent could honestly have none."

25c. CURRENCY RULE ONLY WORKED-EXAMPLES INDIA
Section 12 is written as "If Region = India: [rules]" with no explicit
statement that the same logic must be applied to ANY stated region.
FIX: Add: "This rule applies to every region, not only India. For any
stated region, use that region's real currency and realistic local
price/income bands — e.g. Region = China -> CNY (¥); Region = UK ->
GBP (£); Region = US -> USD ($). Never default to USD as a fallback
regardless of which region rule was most recently defined."

25d. STRUCTURE RULE CHECKS THE COUNT BUT DOESN'T REQUIRE PROFILING TO
BE LISTED
Section 17 checks that "the profiling count matches the actual
profiling block" but doesn't require Profiling to be named and counted
in the structure summary itself. The original bug wasn't bad math —
Profiling was omitted from the summary entirely.
FIX: Add to Section 17: "The structure summary must explicitly name
and count the Profiling block separately from the four behavioral
sections, even though Profiling is not one of the four. A structure
summary that states only a combined total (e.g. '5 sections · 33
questions') without breaking out what each section — including
Profiling — contains does not satisfy this rule."
Add to the automated check list:
"CHECK 37 — The structure summary explicitly names and counts the
Profiling block on its own, separate from the four behavioral
sections."

25e. PERSONA-NAME RULE NEEDS CATEGORY SCOPING
Section 2 locks in exactly four persona names (High-Involvement Users,
Targeted Concern Solvers, Value-Driven Routine Users, Minimalist
Occasional Users) as if they are the fixed global set for ALL B2C
surveys. But those names are skincare-specific concepts. A running
shoes survey correctly invented its own four category-appropriate
personas (Performance-Focused Runners, Value-Oriented Joggers,
Comfort-Driven Walkers, Style-Led Owners) instead of reusing the
skincare names — which is the right outcome, but the rule as literally
written could mislead a future run into forcing skincare-specific
persona names onto an unrelated category.
FIX: Reword Section 2 to say: "The four named personas above are the
approved set for the [skincare / this specific] category. For any NEW
category, define a NEW fixed set of exactly four category-appropriate
personas at the very start of generation, before writing any question,
and hold that exact set consistent throughout the entire survey — do
not reuse another category's persona names, and do not invent a fifth
persona partway through."

FOUND: Every page's running header reads "Coherent Market Insights —

26b. STRUCTURE SUMMARY OMITS PROFILING (confirms Gap 25d is still live)
FOUND: The cover page states "5 sections · 33 questions" with no
breakdown of what the 5 sections are or how many questions are in each.
Profiling is not named anywhere in this summary, even though it is a
real section with its own header and 4 questions.

in a new survey, confirming the master file's structure rule needs the

26c. OFF-CATEGORY QUESTION — Segment 1, Q5
FOUND: "What is the main reason you run?" (train for races / stay fit
/ manage stress / socialize with other runners / save money on
commuting) measures general fitness/exercise motivation, not anything
about running SHOES. The "save money on commuting" option in
particular has no connection to the shoe category at all.
FIX: "Every question must fit the actual product category being
surveyed, not an adjacent lifestyle or activity category. A running
shoes survey may ask about running frequency, terrain, or shoe
priorities — but a question about WHY someone runs (fitness motivation
in general) belongs in a fitness/exercise survey, not a footwear
survey, unless the option set is clearly reframed around shoe-relevant
consequences of that motivation (e.g. 'training for a race' tied to
performance-shoe needs). Remove or rewrite any question that could be
asked identically in an unrelated category survey without change."
